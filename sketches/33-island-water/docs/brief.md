# Brief: Island Water (sketch 33)

## Question

Where does an Aegean island's water come from, who decides, and what would a fairer and
less fragile water future look like? The sketch compares islands, then zooms into
Sifnos, where a centuries-old water commons and a modern desalination emergency sit a
few kilometres apart.

**Scope** (agreed 2026-11-23, details in `docs/scope.md`): every inhabited Greek Aegean
island in the 2021 census, with Crete, Evia, the Saronic islands and Kythera as context
only; present day 1991–2026; traditional systems from living memory, c. 1950 on.

## Why it fits Realtime Weather

The working group asks how weather is monitored, modelled, managed and shown, and who
lives under those decisions. Island water is weather management at its most direct:
winter rain fills or fails to fill cisterns and aquifers; summer heat and tourism raise
demand; when supply fails, a state of emergency is declared and desalination is bought.

## Framing

Zepou & Matsaganis (2024), *Governing the commons on an Aegean island* (ZEPOU24):

- Poulati, a valley on Sifnos's east coast, still shares water from nine springs through
  30+ stone cisterns and furrows on about 15.5 ha, under rules dating from the Byzantine
  era. Water is shared in 12-hour "waters" by weekday; rights belong to the land.
- The authors test it against Ostrom's design principles (a third way between Hardin's
  "tragedy" solutions of state control or privatisation).
- They trace its threats to poorly regulated tourism acting as a "resource curse", and
  suggest tourism be redefined around the island's water heritage.

## Audience and use

A **citizen resource**: islanders, visitors, local officials, students. It should answer:

1. Where does my island's water come from today, and what does it cost?
2. How did islands manage water before desalination?
3. How does desalination work, and what does it depend on (energy, membranes, the sea)?
4. What will islands need in future, and what are the options besides more desalination?
5. How can I add what I know (a cistern, a spring, a story)?

## Biggest gaps (priority order)

1. **Supply mix per island**: which sources, in what share, in what season. Scattered.
2. **Desalination capacity per plant** (m³/day) and age; EMODnet should help.
3. **Current tanker contracts and prices** (only a c. 2019 contract so far).
4. **The University of the Aegean cost study**: press only; find the original.
5. **Demand**: residents vs. summer population, per island and month.
6. **Traditional systems beyond Sifnos and Andros**: cisterns, mills, springs elsewhere.
7. **Sifnos specifics**: desal plant capacities, reservoir volume, borehole data, DEYA
   figures, the 2026 drilling plans.
8. **Oral histories**: who to interview, with consent.
