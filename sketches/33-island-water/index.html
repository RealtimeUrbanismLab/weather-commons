Island Water

Where does an Aegean island's water come from, who decides, and what would a fairer and less fragile water future look like?

33 · Jacob Moe · started 2026-11-23 · draft

Every claim on this page says what kind of evidence it rests on: documented (a public decision or official dataset), reported (news or a study we have read but not checked against a primary record), reanalysed (weather model data), calculated, synthetic (an assumption) or interpretive (our reading).

Event

In June 2024 a desalination unit broke down at Platys Gialos, on the south coast of Sifnos. Some settlements went without tap water for ten to fifteen days, and at some points on the island June use was up to twice the previous August's reported. On 20 June the General Secretariat for Civil Protection declared a water emergency on Sifnos. Serifos, the next island to the north-west, had been under one since 30 April documented.

The winters before

The springs, wells and cisterns of Sifnos are refilled by rain that falls between October and March. In 2022–23 that rain was 66% of the 1991–2020 average for the island; in 2023–24, the winter before the breakdown, 74%. Winters 2022–23 to 2024–25 are drawn in full black.

What happened, 2021–2026

  1. March 2021
    A portable desalination unit of 1,000 m³ a day arrives at Platys Gialos, next to an older 500 m³ unit. A third plant, at the port of Kamares, treats brackish water. documented
  2. July 2021
    The municipality restricts water use because of "prolonged drought". documented
  3. Summers 2021–2026
    Tanker trucks are hired every summer to carry water from the coastal plants up to the village tanks, as they were in 2014 and 2016. documented
  4. June 2024
    The Platys Gialos breakdown; some settlements without water for 10–15 days. reported
  5. 20 June 2024
    At the municipality's request, Civil Protection declares a one-month emergency, for consequences that "may arise" from water shortage. Local reporters found no public notice of it on the island. documented reported
  6. 15 July 2024
    The council discusses proposals against waste, to share water fairly between residents and visitors. documented
  7. 22 July 2024
    The emergency is extended to 22 August, because the works to deal with the shortage are not finished. documented
  8. December 2024
    The council takes up the licensing of swimming pools. documented
  9. July 2026
    Mains water may no longer be used for gardens, yards, cars or pools, with fines for breaches; residents are asked to use their own cisterns. reported
  10. February–November 2026
    A new 1,000 m³-a-day unit with solar panels for Platys Gialos: study approved, €1.05 million of regional funding, works permit, then a tender (€850,000 plus VAT), cancelled on 13 November to be run again. documented reported

A few kilometres away: Poulati

On the east coast, the valley of Poulati still shares the water of nine springs through more than thirty stone cisterns and channels. Each property gets the water for set twelve-hour turns on set weekdays, and the right to it belongs to the land, not the owner. The rules were unwritten until the early twentieth century, when they began to appear in deeds. In 2022 six farmers were still working there, four of them over 75 reported.

In the same summers, water for the main settlements was allocated by municipal restrictions, tanker deliveries and an emergency decision of Civil Protection; in Poulati it was allocated by the weekday turns recorded in deeds interpretive.

Decisions: Diavgeia, the Greek government's public register of decisions (Municipality of Sifnos; General Secretariat for Civil Protection). Press: Ethnos (July 2024), Sustainable Cyclades (July 2024), Kykladiki (June and July 2026), Naxos Times (November 2026). Poulati: Zepou & Matsaganis (2024). Full list under Sources and limits.

Situate

This sketch covers 105 Aegean islands. In October–March 2022–23 all 105 received less rain than their 1991–2020 average, and 100 received less than 80% of it; 28 had a drier winter than Sifnos, relative to their own average. In 2023–24, 104 of the 105 were below average reanalysed. Civil Protection declared water emergencies on five of them in 2024: Serifos from 30 April 2024 to 7 January 2025, Spetses from 4 June to 4 July, Poros from 20 June to 22 July, Sifnos from 20 June to 22 August, and Leros from 25 June to 26 August; Poros again from 27 March to 31 December 2025 documented. Leros and Patmos were declared in drought by ministerial decision in November 2025, and Astypalaia was reported in a water emergency in May 2026 reported. The list is incomplete: one 2024 report counts 14 municipalities in a water emergency.

Desalination over time

The earliest island plant in the records is a 1,000 m³-a-day multi-stage flash (thermal) plant on Syros, online in 1970 and presumed offline by 2020. Of the plants still listed in 2020, those online by 1990 add up to 3,200 m³ a day, and all of them to 94,400 m³ a day documented. Since 2024 the state has granted temporary permits to install and run portable units under a fast-track procedure: 29 decisions, 16,550 m³ a day documented.

What a cubic metre costs

The eight figures below come from four sources and measure four different things: a plant's running cost, its full production cost, the price users pay, and the cost of water shipped by tanker. Around 2012 the running cost of 30 island seawater plants ranged from €0.13 a cubic metre (Leros) to €3.50 (Sifnos), with an average of €0.85. In 2025 the production cost ranged from €2.56 (Mykonos) to €6.69 (Symi); Sifnos's was €3.55 reported. On Chios, a 5 m³ house tank costing about €1,900 pays for itself in 21–36 years at the 2019 tariff; a 24 m³ school tank costing €5,700, in about 14 reported.

Narrate

In June 2024 three conditions coincided on Sifnos: October–March rain at 66% and then 74% of the 1991–2020 average in the two preceding winters reanalysed; the breakdown of a desalination unit at Platys Gialos; and June use up to twice that of the previous August reported. Rain alone does not account for the emergency: in 2022–23, 28 of the 105 islands had a drier winter than Sifnos relative to their own average reanalysed.

Between 2021 and 2026 the municipality added a 1,000 m³-a-day portable unit, hired tanker trucks every summer, restricted use (with fines from July 2026) and put a second new unit out to tender documented. About 80% of the island's electricity comes from an oil-fired power station reported. Across the islands in this sketch, 29 temporary permits for portable units were issued in 2024–26 documented.

With the page's default settings (a 100 m² roof, a 10 m³ tank, three people using 150 litres each a day, a third of it for toilets and garden), a rooftop cistern on Sifnos meets 45–50% of that third over 1991–2026, and 33% in the driest water year, 2000–01 calculated. In Poulati, nine springs feed 21 open-air cisterns (χαβούζες) and about ten troughs (γούρνες); the water is divided into twelve-hour turns, called "waters", assigned by weekday; the right to a turn belongs to the plot, not its owner; and overflows (στραγγίδια) are shared on trust. More than 25 farmers worked the valley in the 1980s; six did in 2022, four of them over 75 reported.

What not to conclude.

That Sifnos was drier than the other islands: by ERA5, 28 were drier in 2022–23. That desalination capacity is water delivered: it is the most the plants can make. That an island with no plant listed has none. That the cistern results are drinking water: they are for toilets and garden unless you choose otherwise. That the costs can be compared one to one: they measure different things.

What this sketch cannot show: water quality, leaks, evaporation from open cisterns, or how people change their use in a dry year. The model has no first-flush diverter and treats every roof as the same. One ERA5 cell stands for all of Sifnos.
