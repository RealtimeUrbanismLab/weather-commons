Tropical Depression

A tropical depression is the first stage of a storm that forecasters officially track and number: a low-pressure system over warm sea with a closed circulation and winds below 34 knots (about 63 km/h). What has to be measured, by whom, before a patch of cloud is declared a tropical depression?

Sources and limits

  • Swirl in the Simulate section: synthetic, made up for this first page. It is not a model of any real storm.
  • Wind threshold (below 34 knots): the usual definition used by forecast centres such as the US National Hurricane Center and the World Meteorological Organization. Source to be added.
  • No observed, reanalysed or modelled data yet. Each dataset added here will list who made it, when, its licence, and what kind of material it is.
