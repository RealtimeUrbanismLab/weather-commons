Prototype, v0: an explorable view of the evidence. The overview is on the main page.

Since 2008 a lift company has covered part of the Presena glacier, above Passo del Tonale, with white fleece every summer. What have nearly twenty summers of covers left, and how far does that match what the operator, the Province and the studies say? Who maintains a public glacier, who pays, and who decides?

1. Event

The Presena glacier sits above Passo del Tonale, in Trentino, on the border with Lombardy. Lifts reach it from both sides. Since 2008, part of it is covered each summer with white geotextile to slow melting on the ski run.1 The operator says the covered area grew from 40,000 m² in 2014 to 110,000 m² in 2020 and 2021, and that snow is also laid on the glacier every winter.1 These are its own figures, shown dashed in the stack.

2. Simulate

Can a satellite see the cover?

Yes. On 14 August 2021, Sentinel-2 shows a bright strip along the ski run. In visible light, fleece and clean ice look alike. In shortwave infrared they do not: ice absorbs it, the fleece reflects it.

How the model works, and what it leaves out

Each day, melt = temperature factor × air temperature + radiation factor × (1 − albedo) × sunlight, when the air is above 1 °C (an enhanced temperature-index model).7 Ice density 900 kg/m³. The seasons control assumes the same patch and the same summer every year: it shows the size of the effect, not its history. Left out: wind, rain, summer snowfall, slope, shading, ice flow, grooming and snowmaking.

4. Narrate

InterpretiveA glacier kept by contract is also a glacier kept by paperwork. Count the plates with nothing in them: most of what the public would need to judge the covers, the area per year, the money, the permit, the fate of the sheets, is not yet in any public record.
